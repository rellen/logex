# logex at d422608: whole-codebase adversarial review

2026-11-01. Four workflows ran 27 reviewers, 63 agents in all, against d422608 (Elixir 1.20.4, OTP 28). Every finding went through four steps:

1. **Find.** A reviewer attacked its scope by running code: fuzzers, metamorphic tests, independent IEC oracles, random walks, real-clock probes and mutation.
2. **Refute.** A second agent, in its own clone, tried to refute the finding.
3. **Counter.** A third agent tried to refute that verdict, whichever way it went.
4. **Tiebreak.** A contested finding rated high would have gone to a fourth agent. None needed it.

Results:

- **203 findings.** 13 were refuted and 190 survived: 12 high, 35 medium and 143 low, each confirmed or partially confirmed.
- **About 60 distinct defects** after merging duplicates. Several were found again by two to four reviewers on their own.

I re-ran seven of the high and medium findings in my own clone at d422608. All seven reproduce.

The maintainer's answers to the questions the review raised are decisions 347–360 in `docs/organisation.md` §7. The fixes are `PLAN.md`'s items RV-1 to RV-8, under "The review after Milestone 6".

## What held

The core held under heavy, varied attack:

- **The evaluator.** A random-program fuzzer ran 1,200 programs × 25 scans. Three metamorphic relations were checked over about 1,300 programs:
  - a program against the same rungs as a block;
  - against the same block as an array element;
  - a jump section against a block whose `cal` is false.

  Independent IEC oracles covered TON, TOF, TP, RTO and CTUD at every width (700 trials), and the shifts and rotates in their source's width (3,000 draws). Nothing came out wrong.
- **The front end.** About 195,000 mutated sources, then parse → print → parse on about 100,000 of them, all round-tripped exactly. `well_formed!/1` accepted exactly the producible trees in 200,000 random ones. Every one of 48,345 diagnostic columns pointed at the right character. Cost is linear, and no atom is made from input.
- **The lone-instance edit.** About 380,000 random walk steps of accept, test, untest, assemble, cancel and restart held:
  - the forecast always equalled the test's report;
  - applying the report's writes reproduced the state exactly;
  - timers followed decisions 339–341, apart from decision 344's documented loss.
- **The runner's scheduling.** 20,000 hostile `get/2` paths, 400 concurrent processes making 10,000 calls, a 1e9 ms clock jump, a trigger toggled 1,000 times, and 11 seeded walks of 400–600 steps. The runner never crashed or hung, and every reply was well formed.

The defects cluster in four places:

- code that re-parses names the lexer has already cut;
- feature combinations added late (bits of members of array elements);
- work the runner does inline against a real-time deadline;
- the online configuration edit's "held" reasoning.

## High: five defects

### H1. A "held" output is not held during a configuration edit
`lib/logex/configuration/edit.ex:756` (`drives/1`) · `b5-config-edit-1` · both checks confirmed it high

An edit can stop driving an output point. When it does, the forecast and every step's report say `{:held, "k1", v}`, and §4.9 and the moduledoc promise that this is what the next cycle's outputs give.

`drives/1` counts a connection as a driver only where the program writes its var_output. A connection whose var_output no rung writes still copies out every cycle, and nothing guarantees it held the global's value. It does not when the candidate does any of these:

- wires the point to a var_output for the first time;
- adds an instance;
- retypes one;
- swaps two connections.

Then the first cycle under test copies that var_output's initial value over the "held" point.

Probe: `k1` was 0 and the stop button still released. The forecast said `{:held, "k1", 0}`, and the first cycle under test gave `k1 = 1`. The opposite direction turns a running contactor off. It reproduces through `Logex.Runner` with the sim. The configuration gives no warning; the program gets only "declared but no rung uses it".

This is the harm decision 23 was adopted to prevent: an output changed under test with nothing in the report predicting it.

### H2. A healthy device can be killed mid-write and left energised
`lib/logex/runner.ex:2716` (`written/3`, `asked/3`) · `c3-io-contract-1` · first check medium, second check restored high

A round writes its devices in name order, and every write shares one real-time deadline, `idle + poll`, set by the one refresh before the writes. Suppose device A's write takes most of that budget but still answers in time:

- device B, written next, gets only the milliseconds left, and is killed although it answers in a few ms;
- B's kill takes its own deadline latch with it, so the trip's zero finds B dead and B never gets its 0;
- B's outputs stay at their last value until a restart;
- the trip reason blames B, `{:io, "b", :timeout}`, though A was the slow one.

It reproduced 3 of 3 runs on the real clock. The code's own rule, in `in_time/6`'s comment and pinned in `trip_test.exs`, is that a device answering late is still written its 0. That rule holds only on the manual clock, where a delay costs no real time. M4-6's sixth verification met this as a flaky test and changed the test.

### H3. An edit's one-shot blocking misses storage bits inside an array of user blocks
`lib/logex/edit/plan.ex:1010` (`word_of/1`) · `a4-warnings-walk-2` · first check medium, second check high

`word_of/1` splits a storage bit's path at its *first* `[`. For a bit inside an element of an array of blocks, such as `gs[0].e[1]`, that gives `gs` instead of `gs[0].e`. Fix F7 / decision 21's "written alone?" check then compares the number of `cal gs[k]` with the sum of every element's bit writes. Two things go wrong:

- **A spurious pulse.** Suppose the engineer removes a stray `otl e[k]` / `otu e[k]` that Logex.Warnings flagged against the one-shot. The scalar block `s1` is blocked correctly. Each `gs[k]` is not, and pulses on the candidate's first scan with no rising edge, a pulse neither program gives.
- **A press lost for nothing.** A body with one more bit write elsewhere blocks one-shots that nothing else writes.

### H4. A warm restart after a plain swap crashes or poisons the instance
`lib/logex/runtime.ex:609` (`warm/4`, `rebased_built_in/3`, `warm_member/4`) · `c4-api-fuzz-1` · both checks confirmed it high; I reproduced it

The plain swap is in the contract: decision 26, the Runtime moduledoc, and §1 "A restart puts the values right". Recompile a program with `var retain k dint 5` added, swap it in, scan, and then `restart(v3, s, :warm)`:

- the restart gives `k => nil`;
- every later entry point then refuses the state, the cold restart included, so only `instance/1` recovers it, losing every retained value;
- an added retained timer that has not run gives `BadMapError`;
- a retained member added to a block, under a false `cal`, gives `KeyError`.

`instance/2` already has the right rule for the same case: a path the state lacks starts at its initial value.

### H5. `Logex.compile/2` raises on source text
Found 13 times by five reviewers. Six distinct roots, each breaking "nothing else may escape the public API". I reproduced 1–4.

1. **A block rung led by a group whose first leg is empty**, `( | xic a ) ote b`, at any depth. `Compiler.element_line/1` has no clause for `[]`, and every block compile goes through `printed/1`. The same rung compiles in a program. Gives `FunctionClauseError`. (`a1-frontend-1`, `a6-compile-scale-1`; `compiler.ex:1683`)
2. **A bracketed literal bit of an instance**, `xic t.[3]` with `t` a `ton`. Gives `Protocol.UndefinedError`. (`a2-compiler1-1`, `a3-compiler2-2`, `a6-compile-scale-3`; `compiler.ex:3706`)
3. **A pulse coil whose pulse bit is a bit of a member of an element**, `osr s lc[1].pv.0`. This is the very form the compiler's own refusal recommends. Gives `Protocol.UndefinedError` in `Walk.bit_name/1`. (`a1-frontend-4`, `a2-compiler1-2`, `a4-warnings-walk-1`, `a6-compile-scale-4`; `walk.ex:185`)
4. **A bit number held in an array**, `xic w.[a]` with `a int[2]`. Gives `FunctionClauseError` in `Declarations.kind/1`. (`a3-compiler2-3`; `compiler.ex:3814`)
5. **`cal` of a nested element or of a name ending in a bit's `]`**, `cal lines[1].gates[0]` or `cal e[0].[n]`. Gives `FunctionClauseError`. (`a2-compiler1-3`, `a3-compiler2-1`, `a6-compile-scale-5`)
6. **`Logex.Array.split/1` cuts at the first `[` and takes the last `]`.** Names the lexer accepts, `int[2].[n]` or `a[1].[2]` as an input key, give `FunctionClauseError` from `compile/2`, `Configuration.compile/3`, `put_inputs/3` and `call/4`. A name ending in `[` gives a raw `binary_part` error from the store and image readers. (`a1-frontend-2`, `b2-resource-3`, `b3-store-image-8`; `array.ex:200`)

The front-end reviewer points at a single cause. Several modules re-split names the lexer has already cut, each with weaker rules of its own: `Array.split`, `bit_number`, `literal_number`, `unsized` and `base_word`. A parsed-name structure produced once would close the whole class.

## Medium: about 20 defects

### Silent wrong behaviour

- **M1. A one-shot's storage bit in a bit of an element's member is accepted.** `ons cl[1].pv.3`, `ons cl[i].pv.3`, `osr cl[1].pv.3 s` and `ons tl[1].pre.3` all compile. The scalar twin `ons c.pv.3` is refused. `shared_bit/3` has no clause for a `{:member, _, element, _}` container. At run time the counter's preset alternates 5/13, and the bit escapes the edit's block list. (`a1-frontend-5`, `a2-compiler1-4`, `a6-compile-scale-7`; `compiler.ex:1003`)
- **M2. A pulse coil's pulse bit through a tag-subscripted member is accepted.** `osr s ta[i].r` compiles, while `osr s e[i]` is refused "so that it is one bit whatever the program does". When `i` moves, the 1 stays in the old element: that lane's counter is held reset for good. (`a2-compiler1-5`; `compiler.ex:1157`)
- **M3. A retained `tof` fires on the first scan after a warm or fresh start.** Every other edge instruction respects `scan.first`; `off_delay/3` does not. A fan or conveyor held on at power loss keeps `.en = 1`, so the first scan sees a falling edge and runs the whole off-delay with nobody pressing anything. The comment at `runtime.ex:4012` still claims "every start and restart puts [`.en`] at 0". Decision 273 covers only a `tof` already in its run-on (`.tt = 1`). (`c7-premortem-plant-1`, `b1-evaluator-1`)
- **M4. A retained trigger's rising edge is lost for good when its round trips before the event task runs.** `cycle_steps/3` records every task's new trigger sample before any step runs, and the runner stops at the end of the tripping step. A warm restart keeps the stored sample (`sampled/3`), and so does the store. If the event task is what clears the latch, the handler is dead until a cold restart. This is decision 313's harm reached by another path. (`b2-resource-1`; `runtime.ex:938`)
- **M5. A rung wrapped onto two lines compiles into a dead rung plus an always-on output.** `xic start xio stop` / `ote motor` gives no warning: the motor runs with stop pressed. A rung that writes nothing has no lint, though such a rung is never useful. An `ons`-only rung does write its storage bit, so it must not be flagged. (`c6-pmi-1`; `warnings.ex`)
- **M6. An adapter that returns a struct from `read/1` or `report/2` is taken as a values map.** A MapSet silently freezes every later input of that device. A URI crashes the device. On the first read, a struct crashes `init/1` and kills a linked host. Sim's `values!/1` and `Logex.IO.io!/1` refuse structs; the device does not. (`c3-io-contract-2`; `device.ex`)

### The runner's inline work against a real-time deadline

- **M7.** Several things run synchronously in the runner after `refresh(state, :idle)` has given every device an `idle + poll` deadline (510 ms by default):
  - the period's image take-out;
  - an edit's test/untest switch, at 4–6× a full scan;
  - a force's fsync;
  - a scan of a large array of instances, which is super-linear from tuple copying: `ton[16000]` takes about 2.3 s.

  So a healthy large plant trips. `var retain tl ton[32768]` trips at its first period on the real clock, and the same plant with no store runs. A test on `blk[32768]` trips `{:watchdog, {:idle, 711}}`. The manual clock, which every store and edit test uses, cannot see any of this. (`b3-store-image-1`, `b4-edit-2`, `c1-runner-rounds-4`, `c7-premortem-plant-4`, `b4-edit-5`, `a2-compiler1-6`)
- **M8. A hung file system hangs the runner for good.** It hangs inside a force's write, or a trip's, stop's or cold restart's store write. Every host call waits with `:infinity`. The device latches still zero the outputs, but stop, restart and status never return. (`c7-premortem-plant-2`, `b3-store-image-3`, `c2-runner-persist-4`)
- **M9. `vetted/3` is quadratic in a device's points.** It builds `changes ++ [x]` inside the runner on every report, first read and reread: 463 ms against 85 ms linear at 16k points. (`c1-runner-rounds-3`, `c3-io-contract-5`; `runner.ex:1801`)

### Persistence

- **M10. A writer killed mid-syscall can finish later into the same `.tmp` the runner's next write uses.** `Retain.written/2` always writes through a fixed `<path>.tmp` with `O_TRUNC`, and a process killed inside a file NIF is reported down while its syscall still completes.
  - The store can end up with a junk tail, which a fresh start refuses, so every retained value is lost.
  - Or it can silently hold the older image while `status/1` reports the new write.

  This contradicts the code's "the store the old or the new". Both checks narrowed the trigger to a stall inside the kernel but before the inode lock, lasting more than 510 ms, which makes it rare. A unique tmp name per write closes it. (`c2-runner-persist-1`; `retain.ex:96`)
- **M11. `start: :cold` writes the store before the runner's name is registered or checked.** A duplicate `start_link` that fails `{:already_started, pid}` has already overwritten the running runner's store, and a crash then restores zeros. (`c4-api-fuzz-2`, `c1-runner-rounds-8`, `c2-runner-persist-7`; `runner.ex:484`)

## PMI: the whole codebase (reviewer `c6-pmi`)

The reviewer also wrote a 3-device host from README.md alone: a retained `ctu`, a retained global, a forced eye, an online preset edit through the runner, a trip, a warm restart, a stop and a fresh start from the store.

**Plus**

- **Architecture.** An interpreter over plain IR data makes online edit a pure function of two programs and a state. An opaque resource gives one pure `cycle/3`. The runner alone owns the clock. Each device runs in its own process and latches to 0 on a missed heartbeat. A trip has one path.
- **Determinism.** Runs are deterministic on the manual clock, and README's outputs reproduced byte for byte.
- **The store.** It is human-readable, and a hand edit rescued a retyped value.
- **Host mistakes.** All 25 or so tried came back as precise `ArgumentError`s.
- **The I/O behaviour.** It is small: an adapter took 10 lines.
- **Naming.** IEC names, each surveyed.
- **Tests.** Seeded contract walks run against oracles that know nothing of logex. Reductions serve as a cost metric that load does not disturb. A golden front-end record catches drift. A fix must fail when reverted.
- **The record.** Every behaviour traces to a numbered decision with options, a recommendation and the choice made.

**Minus**

- **The silent dead rung** (M5).
- **Retained data is fragile across maintenance.**
  - A mistaken deploy drops retained values, and the next write overwrites the only copy.
  - Two runners can share a store.
  - Drops appear only in `status/1`.
- **About 2.4 MB of prose for 34k lines of `lib/`.** That is M16, plus stale "provisional" markers right after decisions 343–345.
- **Frictions found while writing the host:**
  - none of README's 33 examples ship as files;
  - an edit candidate's configuration must have the same file name in a parallel directory;
  - an assembled edit lives only in memory and the store, so a fresh start from the files on disk silently runs the old program again;
  - Sim addresses are lists (`[0]`) while the configuration says `panel.i.0`;
  - README has no adapter example;
  - long rungs cannot be wrapped at all.
- **Code style.** The clauses-only rule yields about 6,000 `defp`s. There are no typespecs and no static analysis.
- **Test brittleness.**
  - Exact reduction pins tie the suite to one Elixir/OTP build.
  - A flake failing one run in seven was "left noted" (decision 337).
  - The contract suite is one 18k-line file.
- **Overflow and faults disagree.** Integer overflow wraps silently while a zero divisor raises a fault, so a host that monitors faults misses overflows.

**Interesting**

- An agent writes the code and the record, and a human answers batched questions. Almost every answer takes the recommendation, though decision 146 went against it.
- Reductions as a pinned per-rung budget work well under load, and trade away portability across toolchains to do so.
- "One rule for each piece of state, with every edit's exceptions listed" keeps online edit tractable. The resume rules of decisions 336–345 show how intricate it gets.
- The runner borrows Logix's ideas (share slots, writes again every poll, a latch on a missed heartbeat) on a VM that is not real-time, and "What logex is not" is honest about that.

## Pre-mortem 1: the project failed by November 2027 (reviewer `b7-premortem-project`)

1. **An upgrade went red with no code change.**
   - OTP 29, or Elixir 1.21 (which `mix.exs` allows), shifts code generation, and about 27 `==` reduction pins fail by one reduction a rung.
   - The reviewer reproduced the shape: a one-line change that makes the scan cheaper turned 6 of 7 tests red.
   - Mitigation: bounds measured within the same run, or counts of logex's own calls, rather than absolute BEAM reductions.
2. **The context window filled up.** CLAUDE.md is about 59k tokens and grew 46% in 8 days. By mid-2027 an agent session starts half full of "since decision N".
3. **The receipts vanished.** When this session's scratchpad goes, so do the mutation tables, the load runs, the failing-test gate and the README harness. "Verify by executing" then cannot be done by anyone.
4. **The flake nobody could name.** Decision 337's failure comes back in CI, which prints "2406/2407 passed", exits 1, and names no test.
5. **The budget ate the roadmap.** Milestones 7 and 8 each need a compiler pass. The "no more than before M5-8" ratio leaves about 5%, and the `==` pins leave nothing. Meanwhile there is still no real I/O adapter, so nobody can run a plant.
6. **The maintainer stopped answering.** There are 51 "provisional" markers in `lib/`, and tests named "provisional" become the specification by default.
7. **A release broke a user.** The first hex publish of "0.1.0" carries behaviour changes, such as saturation becoming wrap, with no changelog or tags.
8. **Drift inside the closing commit.** Decisions 343–345 answered readings that the code, the tests and CLAUDE.md still call provisional. CONTRIBUTING states a map-ordering rule that is false for atom keys since OTP 26.

## Pre-mortem 2: a plant incident in November 2027 (reviewer `c7-premortem-plant`)

2. **"The HMI froze and the controller never answered."** Reproduced: M8. The outputs did go to 0 through the device latches.
3. **"The motor kept running after the runner was OOM-killed."** Reproduced. A card's read was hung when the runner was killed, and nothing kills a hung device then: the outputs stayed on and the new runner could not open the card. (low, `c7-premortem-plant-3`, `c2-runner-persist-3`)

5. **"The year's total vanished after an upgrade."** The dint was widened to lint. Refuted as decided: §4.13 drops even a widened value.
6. **"The count was lost during an edit's test."** Refuted as decided (decision 278).
7. **"After a bad restore the total read 12 instead of 1,234,567."** Reproduced. A truncated store is accepted: a cut at a line boundary silently resets values, and a cut mid-number reads a shorter number. (low)
8. **"STOP did nothing"**, from a forgotten force. Refuted as decided (decision 127).
9. **"Line 1 came back with line 2's numbers."** Reproduced. Two runners on one store path are not refused. (low)
10. **"The motor started on a warm restart"**, through a retained seal-in. Refuted as decided and disclosed (decisions 272 and 303).
11. **"An online edit left the contactor on after STOP."** Prevented as far as announcing it: the forecast and every step report `{:held, "k1", 1}`. Note that H1 shows the opposite case, where the report says held and the output moves.
12. **"A box was counted twice after the crash."** Prevented: `first: true` on every start, and the retained edge memories.
